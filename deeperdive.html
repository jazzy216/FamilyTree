The Migration Story

Out of Sligo. The Davys came from the townlands around Ballintogher and Ballinafad in south County Sligo — famine heartland. James Thomas Davy I was a teenager when the Great Famine emptied Connacht; between the 1841 and 1851 censuses, Ireland lost over 1.6 million people to death and emigration. Like so many, the Davys chose the boat.

Why Tennessee? Irish famine immigrants followed the work inland — most often the railroads. The Louisville & Nashville line pushed through Sumner County in 1859, and an Irish Catholic community had existed there since Hugh Rogan of Donegal built his stone cottage "Rogana" around 1800. The Davys put down roots along Goshentown Road near Hendersonville, a name still tied to the family in local records generations later.

An older American line. The Cummings side tells a different story. The surname descends from the medieval Comyn clan of Scotland — Norman lords broken by Robert the Bruce in 1306. But this family's American chapter likely began generations before the Davys arrived: the strongest record match places Malachi Cummings' birth in Clark County, Kentucky, 1802, and his death in Gallatin, Tennessee — an American frontier family, not recent immigrants.

The union. Around the turn of the century, Cyrus Alvin Cummings married Annie Davy, joining the frontier-American Cummings line to the Irish Catholic Davy line. Their son James Matthew — "Rooster" — tried his luck homesteading at Haxtun on Colorado's eastern plains, where daughter Ann Frances was born in 1921, before drought and hard seasons brought the family home to Middle Tennessee for good.

The Names We Carry

Every surname in the tree, and where it began.

Cummings

Norman · Scottish

From Comyn — Clan Cumming, 13th-century Scotland's most powerful family, named for Comines near Lille. Broken by Robert the Bruce, scattered, endured.

Davy

Irish · Co. Sligo

A diminutive of David, long settled in Ireland. This family's branch: Catholic farmers of the Ballintogher–Ballinafad townlands, south Sligo.

McGarry

Irish · Connacht

Mag Fhearadhaigh — "son of Fearadhach," meaning manly, brave. Concentrated in east Connacht, the same country as the Davys.

Mulloy

Irish · Uí Néill

Ó Maolmhuaidh — "descendant of the proud chieftain." A family of the southern Uí Néill, found through Offaly and east Connacht.

Brookshire

English · Colonial

"Dweller by the brook in the shire." In America since the colonial era — Virginia and the Appalachian frontier before Tennessee.

Moore

English · Scots-Irish

Topographic — "by the moor" — or Irish Ó Mórdha, "the stately one." In Middle Tennessee, most likely English or Scots-Irish stock.

Pender

Norman-Irish

A shortened Prendergast — de Piondárgas — Norman knights turned Irish; or an English occupational name for a keeper of the pound.

Grubbs

English

An English surname of the American South, carried into the family by Margaret Grubbs Cummings, b. 1937.
